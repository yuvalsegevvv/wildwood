# The story of Eldmere (spoilers: for writers and agents only)

The main storyline of the home continent, from the first night in Wildwood to the end of the Emberwake Isles, and the hidden truth
behind it. Read `docs/WORLD.md` first (regions, levels, map). Anything that puts words in an NPC's mouth, names a quest, places a
ruin or explains a monster must fit this file.

**Decided by the owner**: the other continents and their technology, the lost and erased war, the dark energy dumping that makes the
monsters, the hidden watchers, Wildwood's resistance to dark energy, **the player's younger sibling Wren falling sick** as the start of
the adventure, **the reveal at the end of the Amber Reach storyline**, and the delivery: mainly the main quest line, with side content
scattered around the map (section 7). Names, characters, beats and bosses below are *proposals* built on those points; ask before
treating one as final.

## 0. The spoiler rule (read before writing any text)

- Until the end of the Amber Reach storyline the player **never learns the truth outright**. They meet hints, and every hint must also
  have an innocent explanation that the people of Eldmere believe (a myth, a legend, a superstition).
- Before the reveal, no NPC says "continent", "technology", "machine", "electricity", "war with..." or names the Concord. People of
  Eldmere describe outside things with their own words: *a lamp with no flame*, *a box that sings*, *an iron bird*, *black iron roots*.
- Hints grow clearer region by region (section 4). Early ones are easy to miss; by the Rootdeep a careful player can guess.
- The Stormhorn and the Rootdeep are side branches: their hints are rewards for exploring, and the reveal must make sense without them.
- The watchers never act openly before the reveal. Afterwards, looking back, every one of their lines should read differently.

## 1. The hidden truth

### The world beyond Eldmere
Eldmere is one continent of several. Across the Outer Deep lie the others, joined in an alliance *(proposed name)* **the Lumen Concord**.
They live in something like the 1960s of our world: cities of concrete and glass, streetcars and automobiles, radio, film cameras,
propeller aircraft and steamships turning to diesel. **Magic exists there too**, but it has been made into industry: their power grid,
their lamps and their engines run on **light energy**, the element of light drawn out of the world and refined.

Refining light leaves a waste: **dark energy**, as surely as burning coal leaves smoke. Dark energy cannot be destroyed, only moved.

### The war (the "Crown War", about 300 years ago)
Eldmere was once their equal: a civilization of magic and craft centred on **the Crown**, the great mountain where the Crownsea is now,
which was the continent's source of the six elements (`WORLD.md`, section 7). Its people built the Stone Circle, the teleport circles,
the halls under the mountains. The Concord wanted the Crown's light. Eldmere refused, there was a war, and Eldmere lost:
- A Concord weapon of concentrated light broke the Crown. The mountain erupted and collapsed; the sea poured in (the Crownsea, a flooded
  caldera; the Emberwake Isles are its burning southern rim). The same weapon turned the Sunscar's sand to black glass.
- A Concord aircraft brought down in the fighting still lies frozen in a Hoarfrost glacier.

### The Quiet: why nobody remembers
After the surrender the Concord cast **the Quiet** over the continent: a working of magic and machinery together that took the war out
of every mind, and teams that burned the books and chiselled the murals. Eldmere forgot its past, its knowledge and its crafts, and fell
back to villages, swords and bows within a few generations. What the Quiet could not reach:
- **songs**: the herders of Amber Reach kept singing a war song they no longer understand (its words sound like nonsense);
- **spirits and very old beings**, whose minds are not human: Kyuubi remembers; the Heartwood remembers in its own way;
- **stone**: ruins that were missed, deep under the ground (the Glimmer Halls) or under ice.

### The dumping: why there are monsters
Since the war the Concord pours its dark energy into Eldmere, so its own lands can stay clean and bright. It arrives through an undersea
conduit, **the Black Line**, into **the Sink**, an installation hidden in the Emberwake volcano on the old Crown's rim, and from there it
seeps through the old lava tubes under the whole continent (the Rootdeep's Emberdeep).
- Dark energy turns animals and spirits into monsters: slimes from the rot in the soil, goblins from the wild folk, walking trees from
  the forest itself, oni and the nine-tailed fox from the Vale's spirits. **The closer a land is to the Sink, the stronger its monsters**:
  that is the real reason the levels rise around the ring towards the isles.
- It also makes people sick: **the Greying**, a slow sleep that turns the veins grey.

### Why Wildwood is different
Wildwood grows over the roots of **the Heartwood**, the oldest living thing on Eldmere, whose sap is pure light. Its roots drink the dark
seeping up from below and hold it, so Wildwood has only the weakest monsters. People born under it carry that light in their souls:
**the dark can't take root in someone born in Wildwood**, and one of them, with enough strength, could close the Sink. This is why the
Concord watches Wildwood most closely.
The Rootwarden was the Heartwood's guardian: it kept Wildwood closed off (the sealed tunnel) so the tainted things beyond stayed out.

### The watchers
A few people in Eldmere are from the Concord. They live under false identities, report home, study the dumping and the sickness, and make
sure nobody remembers. The people of Eldmere have no idea. *(proposed)*:

| Watcher | Cover | Where | Role in the story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Odran** | a travelling peddler of curiosities | turns up in every settlement, always one step ahead | assigned to watch the player once their light shows; grows to care for them; **tells the truth at the end of Amber Reach** and changes sides |
| **Keeper Maelis** | the keeper of the Stormhorn's lighthouse | Gullrest | runs the relay home: the "dark lighthouse" shines in a light eyes can't see (radio). Loyal to the Concord |
| **Physician Anselm Rook** | Glasswell's court physician, famous for real cures | Glasswell | studies the Greying for the Concord; wants the player's Wildwood blood. The antagonist of the second half |

There may be more watchers than these three (a hook for later).

## 2. What Eldmere believes (the cover story)

Every hint has a matching belief. NPCs speak from these, sincerely:
- **The Sinking**: long ago the gods drowned the Crown to punish the pride of the ancients. The monsters are their curse, which comes and goes like weather.
- **The Silent Years**: nobody knows what happened before, "those were quiet times". Scholars in Glasswell find the gap in their chronicles strange and don't like to talk about it.
- **The glass fields**: where the sun wept.
- **The iron bird in the ice**: the skeleton of a dragon.
- **The stone circles and the teleport circles**: gifts of the old gods ("my grandfather swore it once took him over the mountains in a blink": Oskar, already in the game).
- **The Greying**: an old illness from the sea wind; it never reached Wildwood before.
- **The dark lighthouse**: it stays dark so as not to call the drowned; the keeper is a strange, private person.

## 3. The protagonist and the inciting incident

The player is born and raised in Wildwood's village, with a **younger sibling, Wren** (the sibling is decided; the name is a proposal),
who is the player's only family.

**The night the forest went grey** (the start of the game): a night of grey rain in which the monsters around the village grow bolder.
Next morning Wren doesn't wake: grey lines run up the veins of the arms. The village healer has never seen it in Wildwood; old Oskar
says his grandmother told of a "grey sleep" beyond the mountains, never here.
What really happened (hidden): the dark from below has finally started to overcome the Heartwood's roots. Wren is the first sign that
even Wildwood is no longer safe, and the player has a personal reason to go further than anyone from the village ever has.

The healer slows the sickness with Heartwood sap, but it only buys time. Every step of the journey is a search for a real cure, and
each land gives a partial remedy that holds Wren a little longer (a reason to come home by the teleport circles and see Wren's state
change). The journey ends with the only real cure: closing the Sink.

## 4. The storyline, land by land

Each land has: the story beats (what the player does and why), the cure (Wren's state), the hints (an *object*, a *person*, a *place*),
the meaning of its bosses, and the hook that sends the player on.

### Act I: Wildwood (levels 1-15)
- **Beats**: Wren falls into the Greying; the healer sends the player for Heartwood sap and herbs across the rings of the forest; the
  forest is angrier than anyone remembers. The only healers who might know the grey sleep live beyond the mountains, and the tunnel is
  sealed by the Rootwarden.
- **Cure**: Heartwood sap slows it. Wren wakes for moments, then sleeps again.
- **Hints**: *object*: grey-veined monsters, a few, near the Stone Circle. *Person*: **Odran** the peddler arrives the same week as the
  grey rain and asks odd questions ("Who in the village has never been sick? Which of you were born here?"). *Place*: the Stone Circle
  carries worn carvings of ships with no sails.
- **Boss**: the Rootwarden is found half-grey, maddened: the guardian itself is being poisoned. Killing it opens the tunnel. Its last
  breath: "The roots... cannot hold... for long." (Nobody understands; later it will.)
- **Already in the game and fitting**: Maren: "The forest remembers everything. Some of it is waking up." Oskar: "When I was young, the
  forest came right up to the well" (the Heartwood is shrinking).
- **Hook**: Hanami's shrine is said to know every sickness of the soul.

### Act II: Sakura Vale (16-25)
- **Beats**: the player reaches Hanami. Shrine Maiden Kaede reads the player's soul when it is bound (level 15-16) and falls silent: it
  burns brighter than any she has seen. The shrine's old scrolls name the grey sleep and say "it comes from the sea". The oni of the
  Demon Gate and the nine-tailed fox are the Vale's spirits turned wrong.
- **Cure**: Kaede makes a talisman from the player's own bound soul: its light holds Wren's sickness back (a first hint that the player's
  light matters).
- **Hints**: *person*: Grandmother Chiyo: "When I was a girl the kitsune were our friends. Then one of them grew nine tails" (already in
  the game: the spirits were corrupted, not born evil). *Object*: Odran, now in Hanami, sells a "lantern with no flame" that he takes back
  quickly. *Place*: the Demon Gate is older than Hanami, built of stone no one here can cut.
- **Bosses**: Akaoni (20), the first spirit fully drowned in dark; his fall breaks the ice wall north of Hanami. **Kyuubi** (25) is the
  key hint of the act: being ancient she remembers, and dying she speaks clearly for a moment: "They made you forget. They are still
  watching. Ask the ice what fell from the sky." Nobody else heard it; Kaede says spirits speak in riddles.
- **Hook**: the scrolls name a flower that halts the Greying, the **Frostbloom**, growing only under the Hoarfrost glaciers.

### Act III: Hoarfrost Reach (22-30)
- **Beats**: the player searches the glaciers for Frostbloom with the hunters of Rimehold. The seer **Old Sigrun** keeps the sagas of "the
  winter that came after the burning sky".
- **Cure**: Frostbloom stops the Greying from spreading, but Wren still won't wake. Sigrun: the sickness has a root, "where the earth's
  heat runs black", under the mountains.
- **Hints**: *object*: **the iron bird**, the wreck of a Concord propeller aircraft inside the glacier (Rimehold calls it a dragon's
  skeleton): riveted metal, glass, a painted sun-in-a-ring emblem (the Concord's). *Person*: Odran pays well for any "dragon scale" (hull
  plate) brought to him. *Place*: the frost wyrm (30) nests in the wreck; it is drawn to the warmth still leaking from its engine.
- **Hook**: the glacier valley west leads to Highmark, whose miners dig the black stone.

### Act IV: the Greyspine (26-32), the crossroads
- **Beats**: in Highmark, miners dig **black stone that hums**: dark energy gone solid. They sell it to a travelling buyer (Odran) and
  many are falling grey. The mine's deepest shaft broke into something vast below (the Rootdeep).
- **Hints**: *object*: black stone, cold to touch, that makes monsters come. *Person*: the foreman **Brenna** notes that the buyer's
  coins are too perfect, "every one alike". *Place*: the mountain golem (32) is a stone shell around a jointed metal frame, an old war
  machine of Eldmere's own, woken by the dark.
- **Bosses**: gryphon queen (29) opens the river road south; the golem (32) opens the neck pass west and the deep mine.
- **Hook**: Glasswell's physician is famous for curing what nobody else can. Three roads open (Sunscar, Stormhorn, Rootdeep).

### Act V-a: Sunscar (28-36), the main road
- **Beats**: Glasswell, the oasis city, is the story's first great stage: bazaar, palace, library. **Physician Anselm Rook** examines
  the player's report of Wren and gives a real medicine; he asks, as a favour, for a vial of the player's blood "for his studies".
- **Cure**: Rook's medicine **wakes Wren** for the first time (a reason to go home: a warm scene in the village). It is the best cure
  in Eldmere because it isn't from Eldmere. It needs repeating: Rook keeps the player coming back.
- **Hints**: *object*: Rook's medicine comes in identical glass vials with printed labels; his study has a "cold cupboard" that hums
  (a refrigerator). *Person*: Glasswell's archivist shows the chronicles: 60 years of pages are missing, **the Silent Years**, cut out
  cleanly. *Place*: **the glass fields**, black glass in a perfect circle miles wide "where the sun wept".
- **Boss**: the sand wyrm (36); its defeat reopens **Redgate Canyon**, the road home.
- **Hook**: Rook sends the player south: the herders of Amber Reach have an herb for the relapse (he wants the player away while he
  studies the blood).

### Act V-b: Stormhorn (30-40), side branch
- **Beats**: the fishers of Gullrest ask for help against wreckers and drowned things. **Keeper Maelis** is kind but keeps the lamp room
  locked with "a lock that has no keyhole" (a keypad).
- **Hints**: *object*: wrecks of **steel-hulled ships** with propellers, with cargo of tins of food, glass bulbs, a camera. *Person*:
  Maelis goes up the tower at the same hour every night and comes down with news no ship could have brought. *Place*: the lighthouse
  hums at night, and seabirds avoid it.
- **Bosses**: the drowned captain (35) wears a uniform with the sun-in-a-ring emblem; the kraken (40) at the tip.
- The lamp room stays locked until the end (Act VII).

### Act V-c: the Rootdeep (30-47), side branch, visited in stages
- **Beats**: through Highmark's mine into the dark, following the black stone to its source. Three layers, three truths the player
  doesn't yet have words for:
  - **The Rootways (30-35)**: the Heartwood's roots, grey and withering where the dark rises: *Wildwood is spared because the Heartwood
    has been taking the dark into itself, and it is dying of it.* This is why Wren fell sick.
  - **The Glimmer Halls (36-41)**: ruins of Eldmere's lost age. Murals of a great civilization and of **ships and flying machines coming
    from the sea**; every face of the invaders chiselled away (the Quiet's work).
  - **The Emberdeep (42-47)**: **black iron roots**, pipes of metal running along the lava tubes, carrying the dark in from the south.
- **Bosses**: the root-bound heart (35), the lake leviathan (41), the fire wyrm (47), bloated on the dark from the pipes.
- If played before the reveal, these are the strongest hints in the game; if after, they confirm it.

### Act VI: Amber Reach (35-45) and the reveal
- **Beats**: the herders of Tallgrass and their elder, **Tamsa**, help with the herb, and the player hears the old **nonsense song** at the
  fires. The storm bird (40) and the great beast (45) are the land's troubles. Meanwhile Rook's people follow the player.
- **The reveal (end of the Amber Reach storyline, after the great beast)**: Rook's agents try to take the player (Rook has learned from
  the blood what the player is: a Wildwood-born soul that can undo the Sink). **Odran** stops them, and at the herders' fire he tells the
  truth:
  - the other continents, the Lumen Concord, their cities of light (he shows a **photograph** of his home city and plays his **radio**);
  - the Crown War, the Crown broken, the Quiet, the burned books;
  - the dumping, the Black Line and the Sink in the Emberwake volcano; the monsters and the Greying are the Concord's waste;
  - the watchers, himself among them, Rook, and the keeper of the Stormhorn;
  - Wildwood and the Heartwood: why Wren fell sick, and why only someone born in Wildwood can close the Sink.
  Elder Tamsa then sings the old song slowly, and with Odran's words it finally makes sense: it is the herders' memory of the war.
- **Cure**: there is no cure but one: close the Sink, and the Heartwood can heal, and Wren with it.
- **Hook**: the great beast's fall frees the southern cape; the boats to the Emberwake Isles.

### Act VII: Emberwake Isles (40-50), the end of the continent's story
- **Beats**: the island people of Coralhaven suffer worst: their volcano "breathes black". With the truth known, the player crosses the
  isles toward the Sink. Rook, exposed, flees to the isles to defend it.
- **Hints become facts**: the Sink is a Concord station built into the volcano: concrete, steel, humming conduits, signs in a foreign
  script; the Black Line runs from it into the sea.
- **Bosses**: the sea dragon (45), a guardian of the isles drowned in dark; **the Sink's warden** (50), the volcano's fire spirit fused
  with the Concord machine (Rook's work). The player turns their Wildwood light against it and closes the Sink.
- **Ending**: the dark stops flowing. The Heartwood's roots turn green again, and **Wren wakes**, for good (the payoff scene at home).
  The Quiet starts to thin: people all over Eldmere recall fragments (NPC lines can change after the ending).
- **Epilogue and hook for the next continent**: the Stormhorn's lamp room opens. Maelis is gone; the relay's last message says the
  Concord knows the Sink is closed, and that **its own cities are going dark** (their waste now has nowhere to go). The player relights
  the lighthouse, now a beacon for Eldmere, and on the horizon the first Concord ship appears. The next continent's story begins there.

## 6. Rules for writing hints and lines

1. **Two readings.** Every hint must make sense to Eldmere (section 2) and, after the reveal, to the player (section 1).
2. **Show objects, not explanations.** A glass bulb, a printed label, identical coins, a propeller, a photograph: the player connects them.
3. **The emblem** *(proposed)*: the Concord's sign is **a sun inside a ring**. It appears, unexplained, on the iron bird, the drowned
   captain's uniform, a wrecked crate, the Sink's doors. Use it consistently; it is how attentive players link the hints.
4. **Watchers stay in character.** Before the reveal Odran, Rook and Maelis are helpful, a little odd, never villains on screen.
5. **The monsters' strength is the dark's strength.** Descriptions of monsters can mention grey veins, a black sheen, a wrongness, more so
   in the lands near the isles. Wildwood's monsters look the least touched.
6. **Keep existing lines** (section 4 names the ones that already fit) and add new ones that fit; don't contradict the game's current text.
7. **After the ending**, NPC lines can change (the Quiet thins): a clear reward for finishing.
8. **Update this file** when the owner decides a lore point, and `WORLD.md` when it changes geography.

## 7. How the story is delivered (decided by the owner)

- **The main quest line carries the story.** Every beat in section 4 is a step of one main quest chain that runs through the whole
  continent: Wren's cure is its thread, each land's bosses are its milestones, and the reveal (end of Amber Reach) and the ending (the
  Sink) are its climaxes. A player who follows only the main quest must understand the whole story.
- **Side content is scattered around the map** for players who explore: optional quests from local NPCs, readable notes and inscriptions,
  ruins, wrecks and odd objects found in the world (the iron bird, the steel shipwrecks, the Glimmer Halls' murals, the Silent Years'
  cut pages, Odran's curiosities), and extra lines at the camp fires. Side content adds hints and depth but is **never required** to follow
  the story, and it obeys the spoiler rule (section 0) like everything else.
- The Stormhorn and the Rootdeep are side branches of the map, so their story is side content too (section 0).
- The main quest is separate from the quest board's endless random quests (`shared/quests.js`), which stay as they are.
